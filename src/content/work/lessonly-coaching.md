## The reframe

Lessonly's core was learning content — courses, lessons, quizzes. The next-order question was: *did the learning actually change behavior?* That's a coaching question, not a content question.

## What I shipped

- **The full coaching stack.** Practice attempts (continuous from the Practice product), structured manager review, peer review, and skills assessment — all surfaced in the same workflow as the learning content.
- **The skills model.** Defined the schema for what a "skill" was in the product so it could be measured, coached, and certified. The model is what made the rest defensible — without it, coaching is just feedback floating in the void.
- **First-PM scope discipline.** As the first PM, I had latitude across the whole product. The hard part was choosing not to do things — the coaching surface succeeded because we shipped fewer features deeper, not more shallower.

## Outcome

Shipped. Repositioned Lessonly's value prop from "learning platform" to "performance platform." The skills + coaching model carried into the post-acquisition Seismic Learning identity.
